# MIT Sloan — Master of Finance (MFin)

**Application and all three recommendations due Tuesday, January 5, 2027, at 3 p.m. Eastern / 2 p.m. Central.** MIT Sloan lists March 10, 2027 as the decision date. [Official application instructions](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/mfin/admissions/how-to-apply)

## For recommenders

Current students need **two academic letters from their current institution**, preferably from professors, and **one professional letter from a recent internship**, preferably from the supervisor. The applicant must list that internship in the Employment section. At least one of the three writers must be able to discuss the applicant’s quantitative abilities.

Full-time working professionals need one letter from their current or most recent employer (listed in Employment) and one academic letter, preferably from a professor. The third may be academic or professional. Choose the person who can give the clearest examples of your work. At least one writer must address quantitative ability.

Applicants can preview the recommendation questions and discuss their goals and reasons for choosing MFin with each writer. Family members are ineligible. Each recommender must write their own response and submit it through their personal portal link by the application deadline. MIT accepts a letter or answers to its questions, up to two pages. The committee values examples the writer observed firsthand. Its prompts cover:

- How long the writer has known the applicant, in what role, and how the applicant compares with peers doing similar work.
- An instance when the applicant made a difference to another person or a group, and an example of how they communicate with peers or supervisors.
- How the applicant learned unfamiliar material or carried an assignment through to completion, one area for growth, and anything else the committee should know.

The session mentioned a research supervisor as a possible alternative for a student without an internship. The published instructions specify a recent internship, so applicants considering that alternative should check with admissions. [MIT Sloan recommendation guidelines and prompts](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/mfin/admissions/how-to-apply)

| When | What happens |

| Throughout January | The committee reads applications and expects to email interview invitations by the end of the month. |

## Preparing the application

- **Academic record.** Upload transcripts from every university attended. In the coursework section, identify completed or current classes in linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability, and statistics. Additional certificates and non-degree courses can document preparation in subjects the applicant’s university did not offer. MIT prefers Python proficiency and expects students to demonstrate it before enrollment.
- **Finance knowledge.** The session highlighted familiarity with finance career paths and the ability to explain what draws the applicant to the field. Finance internships and classes in accounting, economics, or finance can provide examples. Applicants coming from another field can demonstrate their understanding of finance throughout their answers. The committee also looks for teamwork, clear written and spoken English, and a willingness to contribute to Sloan.
- **Résumé.** Submit one page in 10-point Times New Roman, as a Word file or PDF. Use reverse chronological order within education, full-time work, internships, other experience, and additional information. For each role, describe what you accomplished.
- **Employment section.** List up to three recent experiences. MIT counts paid, full-time work lasting more than four months after earning a degree as full-time employment. An internship may be paid or unpaid and must be an official role verifiable through the employer’s HR department. Use the résumé’s other-experience section for informal projects or research. Admitted students’ employment is verified before enrollment.
- **Test scores.** GMAT and GRE scores are optional.

### Three short answers

Each response has a **200-word maximum**. The session encouraged applicants to answer each question directly and support it with a specific, verifiable example. An award, ranking, or published mention can help document an accomplishment when relevant.

| Question | What the response should cover |
|---|---|
| 1. Preparation for MFin | Which academic or professional experiences and accomplishments prepare you to succeed in the program? Include relevant achievements in finance, mathematics, statistics, or computer science. |
| 2. Career goals | What do you want to do in the short term and the long term? Explain how the MFin degree will help you achieve those goals. |
| 3. Contribution to the mission | Which personal qualities will help you advance the MFin program’s mission of developing principled financial leaders? |

The admissions team reads the responses alongside the transcript, résumé, recommendations, and videos. The presenters said they look across the application for evidence of interests and readiness, including past success in quantitative courses. Their closing advice was to follow the instructions, use your own voice, and give readers concrete reasons to admit you.

### Two video questions

| | What to record | Timing and format |
|---|---|---|
| **Video 1 — Personal story** | Tell the committee something it has yet to learn about you. The presenter connected this prompt to the former *The World That Shaped You* essay. A formative experience, interest, or part of your background can show how you became who you are. | Up to **90 seconds**, in a single unedited take. Speak directly to the camera, conversationally and without a script. Leave out music and subtitles. A creative setting is fine. Play the recording back to check that your face is visible and your voice is clear. |
| **Video 2 — Spontaneous response** | Answer one randomly assigned, open-ended, nontechnical question. The session examples asked applicants to explain why an achievement mattered to them or describe what they did when a teammate was not contributing. | The recording page appears after the other application sections are complete. You get **5 seconds to prepare, 60 seconds to respond, and one attempt**. Choose a quiet setting before starting. |

## Choosing the 12- or 18-month option

**Choose the duration when applying. Admission is to that option, and students cannot switch afterward.** Starting with the July 2027 entering class, the 18-month option requires a summer internship.

| Stage | Coursework and timing shown in the session |
|---|---|
| July–August | Both options begin with accounting, foundations of modern finance, programming for finance, and either financial mathematics or advanced mathematical methods for financial engineering. |
| September–December | Finance analytics (standard or advanced), corporate finance, ethics and regulation, and financial markets. The session also highlighted a capital markets and investment management proseminar. |
| January | The Independent Activities Period includes Finance Lab, where students apply finance methods to projects. |
| February–May | Electives and a proseminar covering corporate finance, investment banking, and private equity. Students on the accelerated path finish in spring and graduate in June. |
| Following summer and fall, 18-month option | Students complete their summer internship, return for fall electives and remaining degree requirements, and graduate in February. |

Sloan’s mission connects principled leadership and innovation with improving management practice. The MFin curriculum puts that into practice through a quantitative finance core, applied projects, and work on communication, ethics, and regulation. The session described 30+ advanced electives in finance, economics, quantitative methods, and machine learning, plus 200+ electives across Sloan and other MIT departments.

| Concentrations | Certificate areas |
|---|---|
| Capital Markets, Corporate Finance, Climate and Social Impact Finance, Financial Engineering, FinTech | Entrepreneurship, Healthcare, Sustainability, Analytics, Product Management |
