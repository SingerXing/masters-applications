## My notes from the admissions session

**Date:** October 19, 2026

### Curriculum

The program combines four areas into financial mathematics and quantitative finance:

| Area | Prerequisites | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Markets | None beyond interest | Securities (equities, fixed income, options); trading (frequency, exchanges) |
| Statistics | Intro to probability or statistics | Risk (simulation, principal components); forecasting (machine learning, time series) |
| Math | Linear algebra, multivariable calculus | Pricing (stochastic calculus, Monte Carlo); portfolios (optimization, matrix analysis) |
| Computing | Coursework or experience in a programming language | Data analysis (Python, C++, SQL, APIs); performance (backtesting, apps) |

### How to show you're prepared

- Coursework that shows experience in at least one coding language: Python or C++.
- Certificates to make up for anything your courses don't cover.
- Exposure to each of the four areas above.

### What they look for: three categories

1. **Academic.** The main focus is math and computer science. They look at transcripts and the types of courses taken, and at faculty references that speak to academic potential.
2. **Professional.** The program's goal is for students to secure a job, so internship or full-time work experience matters. It doesn't have to be in finance, or in quant finance, as long as it's in a quantitative area. Research is another way to show professional level, along with a letter of recommendation.
3. **Community.** Alumni play an important role, so they want people with leadership skills who will be good community members and a good part of the alumni network. Character counts both in the classroom and as part of the program.
